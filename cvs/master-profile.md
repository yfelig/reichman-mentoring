### UG Labs — Tel Aviv
**Role:** International Projects & Business Development Lead *(title used externally)*
**Also acting as:** Customer Success & UX Research *(internal scope)*
**Period:** May 2022 – Present *(approximately 3 years as of early 2026)*
**Position in company:** First employee. Company grew from 0 to ~25 people, then contracted back to ~7 over the past year.

**What the company is:**
UG Labs is an AI startup developing voice-based conversational technology for children in the education and gaming sectors. The product is a platform for building AI characters that children interact with through voice — combining NLP, character design, SDK infrastructure, and real-time conversation. The technology is built for games, educational tools, and enterprise partnerships with toymakers, edtech companies, and publishers.

**The product at the beginning:**
When Yair joined, the physical product was a box with LED lights and a headset. Kids put on the headset and talked to a voice-based AI character. The experience was essentially a conversational game — the AI would prompt, respond, and guide the child through a game scenario purely through voice. This hardware-first product evolved over time into a pure software/SDK platform.

---

**Phase 1: UX Research & Wizard of Oz (Mid-2022, first ~3–4 months)**

Yair joined as the first non-technical employee. His initial role was to set up user testing sessions — bringing in kids, designing game concepts to try, running the sessions, and writing insights afterward. But there was a key detail: at this stage, **there was no real AI.** Yair was the Wizard of Oz. He was physically behind the scenes, manually operating as the bot — speaking as the character, responding to kids in real time as if it were an AI system — while the team observed the interactions to inform product design.

What this involved:
- Designing game concepts and writing scripts for the sessions — what the character would say, how it would respond, what the game logic was
- Running sessions: logistics, setup, managing the kids during play
- Being the voice of the bot in real time, improvising within a script
- Writing structured insights after each session and sending them to the team
- Thinking from both sides simultaneously: the user experience of the child AND the design of the system

During this same period:
- Learned to write JSON conversation trees — the actual file format used for the first real bot. The structure: an output (what the bot says), a wait for input, then conditional branching to the next JSON file based on what the user said. This was not a training course — he learned it on the job because the product needed it.
- Did QA work: testing the actual product as it was being built, ensuring correctness before it was used with kids

This phase produced a rare foundation: Yair understood conversational AI design from the inside out — as a performer, a researcher, and a system architect simultaneously.
