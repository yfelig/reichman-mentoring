## Questions to ask

These make it a two-way conversation and show strategic thinking:

1. **"What's the unit of output from the generator — a full unit plan, individual lesson materials, a scope and sequence? I want to understand what it actually produces and at what grain size."**
   Shows you know curriculum has structure, not just content.

2. **"You describe three input streams — learning science, usage data, field feedback from SMEs. Which of the three is weakest right now — where are you flying most blind?"**
   Shows you read the JD carefully and think about signal quality.

3. **"How do you handle the tension between district customization and curriculum coherence? Where does that boundary live in the product?"**
   Mirrors their exact positioning back at them. They'll want to talk about this.

4. **"Who are the SMEs in your feedback loop — classroom teachers, curriculum directors, external consultants? How structured is that input right now?"**
   Tells you what the day-to-day actually looks like and how much you'd be building vs. inheriting.

5. **"Are districts using the generator to build new curriculum from scratch or mostly to adapt and align what they already have?"**
   Shows you're thinking about the real use case, not the marketing version.

6. **"You said second-time EdTech founder. What did you learn from the first company that you're doing differently here?"**
   Shows genuine curiosity about him. Founders love this question and it tells you a lot about whether this is somewhere you'd want to be.
